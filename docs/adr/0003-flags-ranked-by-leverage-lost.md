# 3. Flags are ranked by leverage lost, with confidence shown separately

A flag's severity is set by how much leverage the clause takes from the reader —
the ability to sue, to leave, or to refuse a change. Exit cost, expected cost, and
deviation from market norm all inform what a flag *says*, but leverage lost sets
the order and breaks ties. Each flag separately carries its own confidence,
because how sure we are that a clause is what we think it is, is a different
question from how much it costs.

## Why not deviation from market norm

Ubiquity is not safety. Arbitration clauses appear in nearly every consumer
contract and rank first here; a ranking driven by how unusual a clause is would
bury them and surface harmless oddities instead.

## Consequences

- Standard, unavoidable clauses will be flagged prominently, which some readers
  will experience as noise.
- Severity and confidence are two separate signals on one flag. The brief must say
  how a lay reader is meant to combine them, or they compete for the same attention.
