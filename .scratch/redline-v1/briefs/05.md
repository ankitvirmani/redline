## What to build

Above the flags, the reader gets a plain-English account of what the document is and
what accepting it commits them to, so they can orient before reading detail.

Ticket 04 carried a summary field through the analysis seam. This ticket produces it
for real and renders it.

## The constraint that is the whole ticket

**It states only what the document supports, and it never says whether to sign.**

Not in words and not by implication. "This agreement is unusually aggressive" is a
verdict. "You may want to think carefully before accepting" is a verdict. "This is a
standard agreement" is a verdict, and a worse one, because it reassures. So is a
closing sentence that summarises the document's character rather than its terms.

The honest shape is: what kind of document this is, what the reader is agreeing to
do or pay, what the other side may do, and how long it lasts. All of it traceable to
the document. Where the document is silent, the summary is silent.

Write this into the prompt as a constraint the model is given, and then do not rely
on the prompt alone. A prompt is not a guarantee. Decide what code can check about a
summary before it reaches a reader, implement what is checkable, and say plainly in
your report what you could not check and why. Do not claim a guarantee you did not
build.

Jurisdiction neutrality belongs to ticket 08, which places it on the screen. Do not
put an enforceability claim or a statutory right in the summary, and do not mention
where the reader lives.

## How to do it

The summary comes from the same seam as the flags, at `src/analysis/`. Whether it
comes from the same model call or its own is yours to decide: one call is cheaper and
keeps the summary and the flags consistent with each other, and a separate call is
easier to constrain and to test. Justify your choice in your report.

Validate it with `zod` like everything else the model returns. A summary that fails
the schema is a defect.

The stub in `src/model/stub.ts` already reads the fixture sidecars, whose `summary`
field is a real example of the output shape. Extend the stub rather than replacing it.

## Tests, added to `tests/analysis.test.ts` or a new `tests/summary.test.ts`

Deterministic, stub only, no network, no key.

- Every analysis returns a summary. Over both fixtures, including the clean
  document, because a clean document still commits the reader to something and a
  summary that appears only when flags do would be missing exactly where it is most
  useful.
- The summary is non-empty and is not a restatement of the flags. Assert something
  real rather than a length check: for instance that the clean document, which has no
  flags at all, still gets a summary describing what accepting it commits the reader
  to.
- Whatever check you built against verdict language runs as a test, with cases that
  should pass and cases that should fail. If your check is a word list, test that it
  catches a sentence you wrote to be caught and does not catch a legitimate summary.
  Say in the test's name what it does and does not prove.
- A model response with a missing or malformed summary is a defect and does not
  render a half-built result.

Do not write a test that mocks the summary and then asserts the mock came back. That
is the shape of test the standing brief rules out.

## The screen

The summary sits above the flags in the reading field. It is prose, not a card:
`DESIGN.md` has no cards and nothing sits on a background. Body register, 400 weight,
capped between 52ch and 74ch. Use a 2px ink keyline or a field-colour change if a
boundary is needed, and a 1px grey hairline if it merely separates. Zero radius, no
shadow.

This is the Operate register: no display type above 2.875rem, no scattered fragments,
no overprint bar, no load-time snap. Motion 150 to 250ms and state only.

It is above the flags in the DOM as well as visually, so a screen reader meets it
first. WCAG 2.2 AA.

## Copy

The summary itself is model-generated at runtime, so the humanizer skill cannot run
over it. What you can do, and must: run the skill over the prompt's own instructions
about voice, and over every fixed string you write around the summary, including its
heading and whatever appears while it loads. Write the prompt so the output reads
like a person explaining a document to someone who is worried, not like a model
summarising a text. `CONTEXT.md` governs the words: document, reader, consequence.
Say in your report that you ran the skill.
