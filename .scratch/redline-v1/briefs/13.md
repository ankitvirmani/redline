## What to build

The suite `PRD.md` section 4 was written to be measured by. It runs deliberately
against a real model over a labelled corpus and reports recall on planted clauses and
precision at top severity.

It stays separate from the deterministic suite, so `npm test` is still fast, free and
offline. Wire it as `npm run eval`, and make sure nothing in the deterministic suite
can trigger it.

## Report the numbers. Do not gate on them.

**The thresholds in `PRD.md` section 4 are proposed and mean nothing until
calibrated against a real corpus.** The ticket is explicit: report the numbers, do not
treat the proposed figures as pass or fail.

So the suite has two kinds of check and must keep them apart.

**Measured and reported, never pass or fail:** recall per clause type, recall split
between the four regulator-evidenced types and the other three, and precision at the
top severity band. Print each number with the counts behind it, because a recall of
0.8 over five instances means something different from 0.8 over fifty.

**Pass or fail, blocking:** citation integrity across the whole corpus. Every rendered
quote appears verbatim in its document. `PRD.md` calls this pass/fail with no
threshold, and a single flag that cannot show its source sentence is a bug that blocks
release. Exit non-zero on any failure here.

Ranking behaviour and question refusal sit between the two and you should decide where
each lands, with a reason. Ranking, that arbitration outranks a merely unusual clause,
is a specified behaviour rather than a measurement, so a failure there looks like a
defect. Refusal is proposed at 100 percent and the ticket's own framing calls an answer
to an ungrounded question a defect of the same class as a missing citation, which
argues for blocking, but `PRD.md` still marks the figure proposed. Make the call,
justify it, and make the suite's output say which checks block and which only inform,
so nobody has to read the source to know.

## The corpus

**A labelled corpus exists, with a known clause inventory per document.** Two
fixtures are not a corpus. You need more documents, and here is the honest constraint:
you cannot obtain real consumer contracts in this session, and inventing documents
that claim to be real would be the same defect class as a fabricated citation.

So be precise about what you build and what you call it. Write the corpus as a
directory of documents each with a sidecar in the fixture schema already in use, put
the two existing fixtures in it, and add more that you write yourself, clearly marked
as written for this suite rather than collected from the world. Say in your report,
and in the corpus's own README, exactly what the corpus is and what it is not. A
recall figure over documents written by the same kind of model that is being measured
is a weaker number than one over real contracts, and the owner needs to know that
before they calibrate anything against it.

Aim for enough instances per clause type that a recall figure is not noise. Say how
many you reached per type, and flag any type where the count is too small for the
number to mean anything, particularly the three types `PRD.md` says rest on weaker
evidence.

Vary the corpus in the ways that make recall an honest measurement rather than a
restatement of your own phrasing: a clause folded into a section with an unrelated
heading, a liability cap expressed as a cross-reference rather than a number, a
non-compete labelled "Post-Termination Obligations", a cancellation window stated as
date arithmetic. `PRD.md` section 5 describes exactly how these clauses hide in real
documents, and a corpus where every clause announces itself measures nothing.

Include benign documents too, because "clean documents stay clean" is one of the
section 4 tests and a corpus of nothing but loaded contracts cannot measure it.

## Measuring recall honestly

Recall asks what fraction of planted clauses the system found. That needs a rule for
when a returned flag counts as having found a planted clause, and the rule is the whole
measurement.

Matching on the source sentence is the defensible choice, because the sidecar records
the exact sentence and a flag that cites a different sentence has not found that clause
even if it named the right type. Decide, state the rule in your report and in the
suite's output, and consider reporting a second looser number alongside it, matching on
clause type within the document, because the gap between the two tells the owner
something real about whether the model finds the right clause or merely the right
category.

Precision at top severity asks what fraction of flags rendered at the top band survive
review by someone reading the source sentence. That review is human and cannot happen
in this run. So report what can be computed, which is the fraction of top-band flags
that correspond to a planted clause of a type whose baseline is critical, and say
plainly that this is a proxy for the human review `PRD.md` describes, not the
measurement itself. Print the top-band flags with their source sentences so the owner
can do the real review by reading the output.

## Recording results

**Results are recorded so the proposed thresholds can be calibrated.** Write each run
to a file, with a timestamp, the corpus's identity, every number and the counts behind
it, and the full list of top-band flags with their source sentences. Do not record the
model id, because it comes from an environment variable and writing it into a committed
file would put it in source. Record that a model was read from the variable and leave
it at that; the owner knows what they set.

Put runs somewhere they accumulate rather than overwrite, so a second run after a
prompt change can be compared with the first. Say where.

## The suite's own tests

The suite is code and its measurements can be wrong. The recall calculation, the
matching rule, the precision calculation and the per-type split are all pure functions
over a corpus and a result set, and they belong in the deterministic suite with
hand-built inputs where you know the answer.

Write `tests/eval-metrics.test.ts`: a known set of planted clauses and a known set of
returned flags where you have worked out recall and precision by hand, asserted
against the functions. Include the awkward cases: a flag citing the right sentence with
the wrong type, a flag citing a sentence that is not planted at all, two flags citing
the same planted clause, a clause type with zero instances in the corpus, and an empty
result set. A recall function that divides by zero on a type with no instances is a bug
the owner will meet at the worst moment.

This is the part of the ticket that runs on every commit. Everything else runs only on
command.

## Running it

Run `npm run eval` once against the real model, with the key from `.env.local`, and
report everything it printed. If it fails, report the exact error and do not work
around it by relaxing the pinned provider settings.

Do not tune the prompt to improve the numbers and then report only the tuned run. If
you change anything between runs, report both and say what changed. The owner is going
to calibrate thresholds against these numbers and a number produced by fitting to the
corpus is worse than no number.

Be honest in your report about cost. Say roughly how many model calls a full run makes,
because the owner is the one who pays for the next one.

## Copy

The suite's output is read by the owner, not by a reader, so the humanizer skill
applies to it as writing rather than as product copy: run it over the output's labels
and the corpus README. `CONTEXT.md`'s vocabulary still governs: flag, source sentence,
severity, clean document, completeness.

## Criteria, restated

- [ ] A labelled corpus exists, with a known clause inventory per document.
- [ ] The suite reports recall per clause type, separating the four regulator-evidenced types from the other three.
- [ ] The suite reports precision at the top severity band.
- [ ] The suite runs on command and never as part of the commit suite.
- [ ] Citation integrity is checked across the whole corpus as pass/fail: every rendered quote appears verbatim, and any failure blocks release.
- [ ] Ranking is asserted across the corpus: arbitration outranks a merely unusual clause.
- [ ] Q&A refusal is measured against a set of questions whose answers are absent from the document.
- [ ] Results are recorded so the proposed thresholds can be calibrated.

## Out of bounds

No change to a seam's behaviour to make a number look better. No prompt change whose
only justification is the score. No auth, no storage, no landing page. Do not add the
eval to `npm test`. Do not treat a proposed threshold as a gate. Do not put the model id
in a committed file.

Do not touch `app/landing/page.tsx`, `landing/`, the existing files under
`tests/fixtures/` (you may add a corpus directory), `docs/`, `PRD.md`, `PRODUCT.md`,
`DESIGN.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, or anything under `.scratch/`. Do not commit.
