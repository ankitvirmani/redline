## What to build

The reader records conditions they will not accept. Clauses matching one are promoted
to the top of the ranking and marked as hitting something the reader named.

Two halves: the editable list, which persists with the account and is a Supabase
concern, and the promotion, which happens in the ranking seam and is a pure function.

## The rule, and why it is the rule

**Red lines change what the reader sees first and nothing else.**

They do not block. They do not veto. They do not produce a walk-away recommendation.
They do not remove, hide, suppress, collapse, grey out or deprioritise any flag. They do
not produce a count of how many were hit, a score, or a verdict of any kind.

Red lines were conceived for documents you can negotiate. Against an adhesion contract
there is nothing to push against, so changing the order is their only honest job. Turning
them into "this document breaks two of your red lines, do not sign" would reintroduce the
verdict ADR 0007 rejects, arriving through a side door. Watch for that side door in your
own work: a heading that says "2 red lines hit" is a verdict in numeric form, and so is
sorting the whole result by red-line count.

**They change presentation, not detection.** A reader who sets none gets exactly the
same clauses found. The feature is optional rather than load-bearing, and a test must
prove it: run the same document through analysis and ranking with a full set of red lines
and with none, and assert the flag sets are identical in membership. Only the order and
the marks may differ.

## The promotion

In `src/ranking/`, pure, no model call, no network, no database.

A flag matching a red line is promoted to the top of the ranking and marked. Decide what
happens among several promoted flags and justify it: the leverage-lost order from ticket
06 is the obvious answer, applied within the promoted group, because it keeps one
consistent principle rather than introducing a second.

**Matching is the hard part and the brief does not settle it.** A red line is text the
reader wrote, and a flag has a clause type, a consequence and a source sentence. How they
match is your decision and it is a real design problem.

Whatever you choose, hold to three things. It must be deterministic and testable as a
pure function, because that is what the ticket's last criterion asks for and what keeps
this seam model-free. It must not silently fail to match, leaving the reader believing
their red line was checked when it was not. And it must not overmatch so broadly that
every flag is promoted, which would make the feature meaningless.

A defensible design: let the reader choose from the seven clause types when they add a
red line, and let them add their own words alongside, with the clause-type selection doing
the matching and the words being what the mark shows them. That is honest, deterministic,
and it tells the reader exactly what will be checked. If you prefer a text-matching
approach, say how a reader knows whether their red line will actually match anything,
because a red line that silently never fires is worse than no red line.

Do not use a model call to match. This seam has none, and the reason is that ordering
must be deterministic and cheap to assert.

## The mark

A promoted flag is marked as matching a red line the reader named. The mark names which
red line, in the reader's own words, because "you named this" is the whole value and a
generic badge does not deliver it.

The mark is not a severity signal and must not look like one. `DESIGN.md`'s
Identity-Not-Severity Rule already forbids colour carrying severity; the same discipline
applies here, and the mark is a fourth signal on a flag that already carries three.
`PRODUCT.md` records that how a reader combines severity, confidence, completeness and a
red-line mark is open and is the hardest unanswered question on this surface. You are not
going to resolve it. What you can do is make sure your mark does not pretend to resolve
it either: do not merge it into severity, do not restyle severity around it, and do not
let it outshout the severity word. Say in your report what you did and what you left open.

## Tests, `tests/red-lines.test.ts`

Pure-function tests over fixed flag sets and fixed red lines. No model, no network, no
database.

- A flag matching a red line is promoted to the top.
- A promoted flag carries a mark that names the red line it matched.
- No red line removes, hides or suppresses a flag: assert the output set equals the
  input set in membership, over several red-line configurations including one that matches
  every flag and one that matches none.
- A reader with no red lines gets an identical flag set to a reader with them. The
  ticket names this criterion explicitly, so assert it directly on membership.
- Several promoted flags come out in a defensible order, and that order is the one you
  documented.
- Ranking with red lines is still pure: same input, same output, input not mutated.
- No walk-away recommendation, verdict, count or score appears in the ranking seam's
  output. Assert on the returned shape: there is no field that could carry one.
- An empty red line, a whitespace-only red line, and a duplicate red line each behave
  sensibly. Decide and test.
- No network and no database access from the ranking seam: fail if `fetch` is called.

## The list

The reader can add, edit and remove red lines, and they persist with their account.
That is a Supabase concern and sits alongside the library from ticket 11: a migration
under `supabase/migrations/`, row-level security so a reader's red lines are visible only
to them, and each policy commented with the sentence it enforces in plain language.

No Supabase project exists, so this path cannot be verified by running it. Say what you
could not verify.

Red lines require an account. The analysis path must still work with both Supabase
variables absent, and a signed-out reader gets the full result with no red lines and no
degradation. Ticket 11 wrote a test for that; make sure it still passes and extend it if
red lines gave it a new way to break.

## The screen

The rail carries the reader's red lines, per the app-shell brief. Follow the rail's
composition from `DESIGN.md`: the label register for the section label, hairline-ruled
rows, the small ink marker at the left of each row.

An editable list needs an add field, an edit affordance and a remove action, and
`DESIGN.md` has no component for any of them, so you are designing three things. Keep them
in world: zero radius, 2px ink keyline on the field, the label register for its label, the
global focus ring, the ghost button variant that `DESIGN.md` records as defined and unused
for the secondary actions, no icon font, and inline SVG at stroke-width 2 to 2.2 if you
need a mark. Remove is destructive and needs to be recoverable or confirmed; decide which
and justify it, and do not use a browser `confirm()` dialog.

"A reader with no red lines" is a designed state. It explains what a red line is and
what setting one does, which per ADR 0008 is that it changes what they see first and
nothing else. Do not oversell it.

Operate register: motion 150 to 250ms and state only. WCAG 2.2 AA: the list is a real
list, the add field has a real label, edit and remove have accessible names that say which
red line they act on, and the mark on a promoted flag is conveyed in text rather than by
colour or position alone.

## Copy

Every reader-facing string through the `humanizer` skill: the section label, the add
field's label and placeholder, the edit and remove actions, the empty state, and the mark
on a promoted flag.

Two strings matter most. The empty state has to explain a concept the reader has not met,
honestly, including the limit: a red line moves a clause to the top of the list, and it
does not stop anything. The mark has to say "this is one you named" without becoming a
verdict.

`CONTEXT.md` is binding: red line, not preference, rule, filter or criterion. Reader, not
user. Severity, not priority.

Say in your report that you ran the skill.

## Criteria, restated

- [ ] The reader can add, edit and remove red lines, and they persist with their account.
- [ ] A flag matching a red line is promoted to the top of the ranking.
- [ ] A promoted flag is marked as matching a red line the reader named.
- [ ] No red line removes, hides or suppresses a flag.
- [ ] A reader with no red lines gets an identical flag set to a reader with them, asserted in a test.
- [ ] No walk-away recommendation or verdict is produced from red-line matches.
- [ ] Promotion is tested as a pure function over fixed flag sets and fixed red lines.

## Out of bounds

No verdict, no count, no score, no walk-away, no "this document breaks N of your red
lines", in any form, anywhere. No model call in the ranking seam. No change to severity,
to the verifier, or to the clean-document determination. No landing page. Do not resolve
the open question about four competing signals; it is the owner's.

Do not touch `app/landing/page.tsx`, `landing/`, `tests/fixtures/`, `docs/`,
`PRD.md`, `PRODUCT.md`, `DESIGN.md`, `CONTEXT.md`, or anything under `.scratch/`. Do not
commit.
