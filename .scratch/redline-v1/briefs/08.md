## What to build

Three things, and a fourth that is copy placement.

**1. A document-grounded consequence on every flag**, bound to its source sentence.
What the clause does to the reader, in plain terms drawn from the clause's own text.
Ticket 04 may already carry the field; this ticket makes it real and renders it.

**2. External context as a separate field with its own citation**, rendered visibly
apart from the document-grounded claim.

**3. Document-granted exit**: any deadline or opt-out the document itself states,
each with its own source sentence.

**4. Jurisdiction neutrality stated where the reader will see it**, not buried, plus
the standing statement that Redline describes documents and does not give legal
advice.

## The two-tier rule, which is the point

"You cannot join a class action against them" is in the document. "Consumers win only
9% of arbitrations" is CFPB data. The second is what makes the first land, and
presenting them in one voice would breach the rule the product rests on.

**They are two different types, not one string with a formatting convention.** The
spec says so explicitly. Model them as distinct fields with distinct types, so that no
render path can accidentally concatenate them and no future change can blur them.
A consequence carries its source sentence. An external fact carries its own citation
and is marked as coming from outside the document.

## The external fact base

**Curated, sourced, reviewable, and not from model recall.** Its accuracy is ours
rather than the document's, which is why it needs a store we can review rather than
being generated per request.

Put it at `src/analysis/external-context.ts`, or as data under `src/domain/` with a
thin accessor, whichever you can defend. Each entry carries the fact as the reader
will read it, the source's name, the URL, and the clause type or types it attaches
to. Draw only from the sourced list in `PRODUCT.md` under "Evidence on Hand" and the
citations in `PRD.md` section 5. Invent nothing. Do not add a fact you found
elsewhere, however true, because the review that makes this store trustworthy has not
happened for it.

Facts available to you, from those two files: the CFPB arbitration study findings,
the Center for Justice and Democracy figures on forced arbitration and arbitration
outcomes, the FTC negative-option complaint figures and the HelloFresh and TFG
Holding settlements, the FTC non-compete rule figures and its being blocked in August
2024, and the CFPB credit-card late-fee figures. Some of the seven clause types have
no sourced external fact at all. Leave those without one. A flag with no external
context is correct and common; a flag with an invented one is the defect this ticket
exists to prevent.

**A test asserts every external citation resolves to a real source stating that
fact.** Resolving cannot mean fetching a URL, because the deterministic suite makes no
network call. So decide what "resolves" means in code and be honest about the gap:
a citation whose URL is well formed, whose source is named, and whose text matches the
verbatim wording recorded in `PRODUCT.md` or `PRD.md` is checkable without a network
call, and that is a real check. Whether the URL is live is not, and you should say so
in your report rather than implying you verified it. Consider a separate, deliberately
run script that does check the URLs, and say whether you wrote one.

## Exit, and what it may never contain

Exit information covers only deadlines and opt-outs the document states, each with a
source sentence verified by the same code path a flag's source sentence uses. The
arbitration clause in `tests/fixtures/adhesion-contract.txt` grants an opt-out window
on purpose, and its sidecar records the sentence, so you have real material.

**No statutory right appears anywhere in a flag.** Not a state cancellation law, not
a cooling-off period, not a right to sue, not a consumer-protection statute, not "you
may still have rights under local law". Even where the reader holds them. ADR 0005 and
ADR 0007 both say so, and the reason is that asserting one moves liability onto us
and breaks the rule that we state only what the document says.

Write a test that proves this rather than trusting the prompt: assert that no exit
text and no consequence in the output of either fixture contains statutory language,
using a list of terms you can defend, and say in your report what the list does and
does not catch.

## Jurisdiction neutrality and the legal-advice statement

Both are stated where the reader will see them, not buried. `PRODUCT.md` records that
every analysis carries a standing statement that Redline describes documents and does
not give legal advice, confirmed by the owner, and ADR 0005 records that silence
invites the reader to assume a flag is legally operative where they live.

"Not buried" means a reader who reads the flags encounters it without going looking.
Decide where, and defend it. A once-per-analysis statement near the top of the result
is stronger than a footer and weaker than a line on every flag; there is a real
judgement here about whether repeating it on each flag reads as honest or as noise.
Make the call, write the reason in your report, and put a test on its presence so it
cannot quietly disappear.

No sign or don't-sign verdict appears in a flag. Not in the consequence, not in the
exit, not in the external context, not by implication. Test it the same way you test
the statutory language.

## Tests, `tests/flag-content.test.ts`

Deterministic, stub only, no network.

- Every flag from both fixtures carries a consequence, and the source sentence that
  consequence is bound to appears verbatim in the extracted text. The binding, not
  just the presence.
- External context, where present, is a separate field carrying a source name and a
  URL, and is never concatenated into the consequence. Assert the consequence string
  does not contain the external fact's text.
- Every external citation in the fact base is well formed and its wording matches the
  sourced text recorded in `PRODUCT.md` or `PRD.md`. Over the whole store, not over
  the facts one fixture happens to use.
- Every exit carries a source sentence that verifies verbatim. The arbitration
  opt-out from the fixture is the concrete case.
- No statutory language in any consequence or exit, over both fixtures and over the
  whole fact base.
- No verdict language in any flag field, over both fixtures.
- The jurisdiction-neutrality statement and the legal-advice statement are present on
  a rendered analysis.
- A flag with no external context renders correctly and is not treated as incomplete.
  This is the common case and it must not look like a gap.

## The screen

`DESIGN.md` gives you the shape for the two tiers and you should use it rather than
inventing one. An open flag's body is white, zero radius, a 2px ink keyline on three
sides with the top border removed so it reads as continuous with the bar above it.
**Inset blocks inside it, which is exactly what external context is, use a cool-grey
fill with no border.** That inset grey block is the visible separation ADR 0007 asks
for, already in the system.

The exit is a line attached to something, so it takes the tab-bar motif: the small ink
tab that says this line belongs to the clause above it.

The document-grounded claim leads and the external fact sits beneath it. That order is
in the ADR and it is not a layout preference.

Mark the external fact as external in words, not only by its grey field, because
colour and fill cannot carry meaning alone under the accessibility commitment and
because a reader who skims must not mistake a CFPB statistic for something their
contract says. Name the source where the reader can see it.

Operate register: no display type above 2.875rem, no scattered fragments, no
overprint bar, no load-time snap, motion 150 to 250ms and state only. Zero radius, no
shadow except the single zero-blur left-edge tab the system permits.

WCAG 2.2 AA, and this surface is where it bites hardest: the consequence, the source
sentence, the external fact and the exit are four related pieces of text and a screen
reader has to be able to tell which is which and what each is attached to. Use real
headings and real relationships.

## Copy

Every reader-facing string goes through the `humanizer` skill: the label that marks
external context, the exit's wording, the neutrality statement, the legal-advice
statement, the source attributions, and the fact strings in the store, which a reader
reads directly. `CONTEXT.md` is binding: consequence, not impact or effect or
implication. External context, not background or supporting evidence. Source sentence,
not citation or excerpt.

The neutrality statement deserves particular care. It has to be plain enough that a
worried non-lawyer takes it in, and specific enough to actually warn: Redline says
what a clause says and does, and does not say whether it holds where the reader lives.
Say in your report that you ran the skill.
